# A / V001 — Gene-delivery concept investigation

## Research question

Could a protective biological instruction plausibly be delivered to relevant airway cells? This is a feasibility and safety question, not a designed or working gene therapy.

## Target airway cell

Which airway cell populations would need to be reached remains unresolved. Candidate populations for literature review include surface airway epithelial cells, mucus-producing goblet cells and other relevant airway cell populations. Cell targeting is a research question, not an assumed capability.

## Broad biological-message hypotheses

The review considered broad, literature-backed pathway hypotheses only:

- **TSLP and IL-33:** early epithelial alarm signals that can initiate or amplify type-2 inflammation.
- **IL-4 and IL-13:** type-2 cytokine signalling involved in allergic inflammation, mucus production and airway responsiveness.
- **IL-5:** supports eosinophil development and survival.
- **JAK–STAT and NF-κB:** intracellular signalling networks connecting inflammatory messages to cellular responses.

No sequence, genetic instruction, construct, promoter, vector architecture or molecular design was specified.

## Delivery barriers

The concept review identified airway mucus, mucociliary clearance, epithelial tight junctions, receptor availability and immune responses as barriers that could prevent delivery or make it unpredictable.

## Safety questions

- Off-target delivery and effects in unintended cells.
- Immune reactions to the delivery concept or its payload.
- Duration and reversibility of any expression.
- Preservation of normal airway defence and epithelial function.
- Whether repeated delivery would be feasible or would create additional immune or safety issues.

## Potential research readouts

Inflammatory signalling, epithelial integrity, mucus-related markers and airway smooth-muscle responses were identified as possible research readouts. These are not equivalent to prevention of real asthma attacks.

## A-stage references

- Pulmonary gene delivery — realities and possibilities: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7876645/
- Effective viral-mediated lung gene therapy — airway barriers: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10284697/
- NICE NG244 asthma guidance: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng244
- Mechanisms and therapeutic strategies of asthma (2026 review): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13454633/

**A-stage outcome:** Concept review generated. No biological vector was constructed or tested. This is an educational evidence investigation, not a calibrated asthma simulator, patient-specific endotype test, gene-therapy design or clinical recommendation. No attack-reduction percentages were generated.

# B / V001 — Connected asthma pathway investigation

## Research question

How do airway triggers, immune signalling and airway structure connect to contribute to asthma symptoms and exacerbations? The map deliberately includes type-2-high and non-type-2 biology rather than assuming one asthma subtype.

## Scope and totals

- **18 pathway topics**
- **24 connected relationships**
- **11 research areas**
- Full pathway sweep generated.
- Qualitative evidence review only; no attack-reduction score calculated.

## Research areas and evidence tiers

| Research area | Topics | Evidence tier |
|---|---|---|
| Airway barrier and trigger sensing | Airway epithelial barrier | Well-established biology |
| Early alarm signals | TSLP, IL-33 and IL-25 | Well-established biology |
| Immune activation | Dendritic cells and Th2 cells; ILC2 | Well-established biology |
| Type-2 cytokine signalling | IL-4; IL-5 and eosinophils; IL-13 | Well-established biology |
| Allergic effector response | IgE and mast cells | Well-established biology |
| Airway narrowing and sensitivity | Leukotrienes/mediators; smooth-muscle contraction; airway-wall swelling | Well-established biology |
| Mucus and airway clearance | Goblet cells and mucus plugging | Well-established biology |
| Non-type-2 and mixed inflammation | Neutrophils, Th17 and IL-17-associated signals | Context-dependent research |
| Intracellular signal networks | JAK–STAT; NF-κB | Well-established biology |
| Long-term airway changes | Airway remodelling and fibrosis | Well-established biology |
| Exacerbation and attack dynamics | Triggers; asthma exacerbation | Well-established biology |

## All 18 pathway topics

1. **Airway epithelial barrier:** airway lining senses allergens, viruses, smoke, pollution and irritants while protecting underlying tissue; barrier injury or activation can initiate alarm signalling and influence airway defence. Relevant across asthma patterns, with varying contributions.
2. **TSLP, IL-33 and IL-25:** epithelial alarmins can activate dendritic cells, ILC2s and other immune cells, initiating or amplifying inflammation. Important in several type-2 pathways, but not dominant in every pattern.
3. **Dendritic cells and Th2 cells:** dendritic cells present allergen information and can promote type-2 helper T-cell responses, linking airway exposures to IL-4, IL-5 and IL-13. Most relevant to allergic/type-2 responses.
4. **Group 2 innate lymphoid cells (ILC2):** resident immune cells respond rapidly to epithelial alarmins and release type-2 cytokines, amplifying IL-5 and IL-13, including without conventional allergen-specific adaptive responses. Can contribute to allergic and non-allergic type-2-high asthma.
5. **IL-4 signalling:** supports type-2 immune differentiation and IgE class switching in B cells, linking allergic sensitisation to IgE and mast-cell activation. Type-2-high context; not universal.
6. **IL-5 and eosinophils:** IL-5 supports eosinophil development, recruitment and survival; eosinophilic inflammation can contribute to airway injury and exacerbations. Key in eosinophilic/type-2-high asthma; blood eosinophils are an imperfect marker.
7. **IL-13 signalling:** influences mucus-producing cells, airway responsiveness and structural airway changes; can contribute to goblet-cell changes, mucus hypersecretion and bronchial hyperresponsiveness. Type-2-high context; strength varies.
8. **IgE and mast cells:** IgE-bound mast cells can release mediators after allergen cross-linking; histamine, leukotrienes and other mediators can contribute rapidly to bronchoconstriction and symptoms. More relevant to allergic asthma; not required for every attack.
9. **Leukotrienes and other inflammatory mediators:** affect airway smooth muscle, vessels, nerves and mucus secretion, contributing to narrowing, swelling, mucus and heightened responsiveness. Multiple mediator routes operate across phenotypes.
10. **Airway smooth-muscle contraction:** surrounding muscle tightens and reduces airway opening; a major immediate contributor to reversible airflow obstruction during an attack. Common downstream pathway despite different upstream triggers.
11. **Goblet cells and mucus plugging:** goblet-cell expansion and excess mucus can obstruct smaller airways; IL-13 and epithelial inflammation can promote mucus changes, while impaired clearance can worsen obstruction. Varies between people; important in some severe disease.
12. **Airway-wall swelling:** inflammation increases vascular leakage and airway-wall oedema, adding to narrowing alongside muscle contraction and mucus. Can occur across inflammatory patterns.
13. **Neutrophils, Th17 and IL-17-associated signals:** some asthma involves neutrophilic or mixed inflammation rather than dominant eosinophilic type-2 biology; may contribute to persistent inflammation, airway changes and variable treatment response. Context-dependent research; mechanisms remain heterogeneous.
14. **JAK–STAT signalling:** intracellular routes used by several cytokine receptors; can transmit cytokine messages into gene-expression changes. The specific cytokine and cell context matter; not a single asthma switch.
15. **NF-κB inflammatory signalling:** transcriptional network activated by inflammatory and stress signals; regulates inflammatory gene expression and epithelial/immune responses. Broad inflammatory pathway, not asthma-specific.
16. **Airway remodelling and fibrosis:** persistent inflammation can be associated with thicker airway walls, smooth-muscle changes and altered tissue structure, contributing to persistent airflow limitation and heightened responsiveness. Varies substantially; not everyone develops the same changes.
17. **Triggers:** viruses, allergens, smoke, pollution and other exposures can irritate or infect airways and activate different pathways, initiating epithelial alarm signalling, mediator release and worsening obstruction. Relevant triggers differ between individuals.
18. **Asthma exacerbation (attack):** worsening episode involving variable airflow obstruction and respiratory symptoms; may involve changing mixtures of smooth-muscle tightening, swelling, mucus and inflammation. Not reducible to one cytokine or immune-cell type.

## All 24 connected relationships

1. Airway epithelial barrier → TSLP, IL-33 and IL-25: airway stress can prompt epithelial alarm signalling.
2. TSLP, IL-33 and IL-25 → dendritic cells and Th2 cells: alarmins can activate dendritic-cell pathways.
3. TSLP, IL-33 and IL-25 → ILC2: alarmins can activate resident ILC2 cells.
4. Dendritic cells and Th2 cells → IL-4: Th2 responses can produce IL-4.
5. Dendritic cells and Th2 cells → IL-5/eosinophils: Th2 responses can produce IL-5.
6. Dendritic cells and Th2 cells → IL-13: Th2 responses can produce IL-13.
7. ILC2 → IL-5/eosinophils: ILC2 cells can amplify IL-5 signalling.
8. ILC2 → IL-13: ILC2 cells can amplify IL-13 signalling.
9. IL-4 → IgE and mast cells: IL-4 supports IgE class switching.
10. IgE and mast cells → inflammatory mediators: allergen cross-linking of IgE can trigger mast-cell mediator release.
11. IL-5/eosinophils → exacerbation: eosinophilic inflammation can contribute to flare risk in susceptible people.
12. IL-13 → goblet cells and mucus plugging: IL-13 can promote mucus-producing cell changes.
13. IL-13 → airway smooth-muscle responsiveness: IL-13 can contribute to airway hyperresponsiveness.
14. Inflammatory mediators → smooth-muscle contraction: mediators can cause contraction.
15. Inflammatory mediators → airway-wall swelling: mediators can increase swelling.
16. Inflammatory mediators → goblet cells/mucus: mediators can increase mucus secretion.
17. Neutrophils/Th17/IL-17 → exacerbation: neutrophilic or mixed inflammation can contribute to some exacerbations.
18. JAK–STAT → IL-5/eosinophils: cytokine receptor signalling can use JAK–STAT routes.
19. NF-κB → airway epithelial barrier: stress and inflammatory signals can activate NF-κB in airway cells.
20. Goblet cells/mucus plugging → exacerbation: mucus plugging can worsen airflow obstruction.
21. Smooth-muscle contraction → exacerbation: tightening is a major component of acute airflow obstruction.
22. Airway-wall swelling → exacerbation: swelling contributes to narrowing.
23. Triggers → airway epithelial barrier: exposures can injure or activate the barrier.
24. Exacerbation/chronic inflammation → airway remodelling: repeated or chronic inflammation can be associated with remodelling over time.

# C / V001 — Controlled asthma prevention feasibility experiment

## Research question

In an abstract teaching model, how do assumed pathway modulation, target selectivity, airway access, preservation of normal airway defence, reversibility and off-target burden affect a pathway-pressure index and a separate airway-defence risk index?

## Default experimental assumptions

| Parameter | Setting | Meaning in this teaching model |
|---|---:|---|
| Hypothetical pathway modulation | 40 / 100 | Assumed strength of signal change |
| Target selectivity | 70 / 100 | Assumed selectivity for intended cell population |
| Airway access | 55 / 100 | Abstract allowance for mucus, clearance and epithelial barriers |
| Normal airway-defence preservation | 85 / 100 | Assumed preservation of protective airway functions |
| Reversibility | 80 / 100 | Assumed ability to reverse an unwanted effect |
| Off-target burden | 15 / 100 | Assumed unintended-effect burden |

These are abstract assumptions, not doses, delivery settings or biological measurements.

## Five experimental arms

| Arm | Purpose |
|---|---|
| A — Baseline | No delivery concept; reference airway-inflammatory state. |
| B — Sham control | Conceptual delivery event without a pathway effect. |
| C — Non-targeting concept control | Conceptual delivery with no intended pathway modulation; tests target-dependence of assumed benefit. |
| D — Targeted concept scenario | Hypothetical modulation of one selected inflammatory signal, conditional on access and selectivity. |
| E — Safety-stress scenario | Same concept under increased off-target burden and reduced airway-defence preservation. |

## Replication

100 deterministic synthetic replicates per arm; five arms; **500 calculations** in total. The repeats are repeated calculations, not independent biological samples.

## Recorded results

| Arm | Pathway-pressure index | Assumed signal shift | Airway-defence risk index |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A — Baseline | 50.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| B — Sham control | 49.99 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| C — Non-targeting concept control | 62.75 | 0.00 | 12.75 |
| D — Targeted concept scenario | 59.68 | 3.08 | 12.75 |
| E — Safety-stress scenario | 64.14 | 3.08 | 17.21 |

All values are invented dimensionless teaching indices. Lower pathway-pressure index is not a prediction of fewer asthma attacks.

## Model bookkeeping and equations

The implementation uses the following abstract bookkeeping:

- Fractions: pathway modulation = 0.40; access = 0.55; selectivity = 0.70; defence preservation = 0.85; reversibility = 0.80; off-target burden = 0.15.
- Assumed signal shift on concept arms = pathway modulation × airway access × target selectivity × 20. With the defaults: 0.40 × 0.55 × 0.70 × 20 = **3.08**.
- Airway-defence risk = ((1 − defence preservation) × 35 + off-target burden × 30 + (1 − reversibility) × 15). With the defaults: (0.15 × 35) + (0.15 × 30) + (0.20 × 15) = **12.75**.
- Safety-stress arm multiplies this risk bookkeeping by 1.35: 12.75 × 1.35 = **17.2125**, displayed as **17.21**.
- Pathway-pressure index = clamp(50 − assumed signal shift + airway-defence risk + deterministic synthetic noise, 0–100).
- Per-replicate noise = ((((replicate index × 37 + arm index × 19) mod 17) − 8) / 20).

These equations are deliberately invented and are not fitted to airway-cell, animal, clinical or published efficacy data. The small variation between arms includes the deterministic noise schedule.

## Interpretation of this run

- D is 3.07 index points lower than C on the pathway-pressure index (59.68 versus 62.75).
- C and D have the same airway-defence risk index (12.75).
- E has both a higher pathway-pressure index (64.14) and higher defence-risk index (17.21).
- The model therefore illustrates a modest assumed signal shift while retaining a delivery-associated safety burden.
- This is conditional on invented assumptions and equations. It is not a biological discovery, an efficacy estimate, evidence of successful delivery, or an estimate of fewer attacks.

## Pre-specified interpretation checks

- **Specificity:** targeted concept should differ from non-targeting control only because of the explicitly stated pathway assumption.
- **Safety:** any apparent pathway benefit must be considered alongside off-target burden and preservation of normal airway defence.
- **Robustness:** vary access and selectivity assumptions; avoid cherry-picking a favourable setting.
- **Generalisability:** type-2-high, type-2-low/neutrophilic and mixed patterns must not be treated as interchangeable.
- **Evidence gap:** real prevention would require validated preclinical and clinical evidence, not this synthetic model.

## Next model-development question

Audit the C-stage equations to separate the assumed effect of pathway modulation from the assumed burden of conceptual delivery. Test whether the non-targeting control's increased pressure and risk are intentional assumptions or an artefact of the model design. A later multi-pathway study could compare epithelial alarm signalling, type-2 cytokines, eosinophil-supporting signals, mucus/clearance, airway hyperresponsiveness and broad intracellular signalling as distinct research hypotheses, with separate outcome measures and explicit phenotype contexts. Keep delivery feasibility and biological-pathway hypotheses as separate dimensions.
